## What makes Mirakl different

Mirakl is not a general-purpose cloud infrastructure provider like AWS or Azure; it is a specialized SaaS platform dedicated to the **multi-sided marketplace economy**. While hyperscalers provide the compute and storage primitives, Mirakl provides the business logic, orchestration, and AI-driven tools required to run a functional online marketplace. Its core differentiator is **Mirakl Nexus**, described as an "Agentic Commerce Brain" that connects shoppers, merchants, and agentic platforms to enable autonomous discovery and transactions.

Unlike generic e-commerce platforms (e.g., Shopify, Magento) that primarily serve single-vendor retail, Mirakl is engineered for **marketplace models** (B2C and B2B). It handles the complex three-way relationship between the platform owner, third-party sellers, and the end consumer. The architecture is designed for extreme scale, managing over 600 million SKUs and 1 billion inventory updates daily, ensuring 100% uptime during peak events like Black Friday.

The platform also integrates **Retail Media** (Mirakl Ads) directly into the commerce workflow, allowing retailers to monetize their inventory and shopper data through AI-optimized advertising. This vertical integration of commerce, advertising, and logistics (via Mirakl Payout and Connect) creates a closed-loop ecosystem that generic cloud providers cannot replicate out-of-the-box.

## Pricing model

Mirakl operates on a **custom enterprise subscription** model. Specific pricing tiers, per-transaction fees, or usage-based costs are not publicly listed on their website. Pricing is determined based on factors such as the number of SKUs, transaction volume, number of sellers, and the specific modules selected (Marketplace, Ads, Connect, etc.).

This stands in contrast to typical cloud pricing models that offer transparent, self-serve metered billing. For Mirakl, the "infrastructure" cost is bundled into a negotiated commercial contract, often involving implementation and professional services fees.

## When it fits

*   **Enterprise Retailers launching a Marketplace:** Companies like Best Buy, Carrefour, or H&M that want to offload inventory to third-party sellers without building the complex backend logic themselves.
*   **B2B Distribution Platforms:** Organizations needing robust seller onboarding, catalog management, and complex pricing rules for wholesale markets.
*   **Retail Media Network Operators:** Brands looking to monetize their first-party shopper data through onsite advertising without building a separate ad tech stack.
*   **Multi-Channel Sellers:** Businesses using Mirakl Connect to syndicate catalogs across Amazon, eBay, and their own marketplace simultaneously.

## When it doesn't

*   **General Compute/Storage Needs:** Mirakl does not provide virtual machines, object storage, or database services.
*   **Single-Vendor E-commerce:** If you are running a traditional direct-to-consumer store without third-party sellers, a headless commerce solution or SaaS builder is more appropriate.
*   **Startups seeking self-serve onboarding:** Mirakl is an enterprise-grade solution requiring sales engagement and implementation, not a DIY low-cost tool.

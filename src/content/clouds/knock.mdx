## What makes Knock different

Knock addresses the fragmentation inherent in building notification systems from scratch. Instead of managing separate integrations for SendGrid, Twilio, Firebase, and custom in-app UIs, Knock provides a unified API and dashboard to orchestrate messages across all channels. It acts as a middleware layer that sits between your application's business logic and external delivery providers, handling routing, batching, and user preferences centrally.

A key differentiator is its "Infrastructure as Code" approach via the Knock CLI and Git integration. Developers can define workflows, templates, and channels in code, enabling version control, CI/CD pipelines, and collaborative reviews. This contrasts sharply with traditional CPaaS models where logic is often scattered across backend code and disjointed dashboard configurations. Additionally, Knock’s "Agents" feature allows for AI-driven workflow generation and email composition, reducing the manual effort required to maintain complex messaging journeys.

The platform also emphasizes user-centric controls through built-in preference centers, allowing end-users to manage their notification settings across channels. This reduces churn and spam complaints by giving users granular control over what they receive, a feature that typically requires significant custom development when using raw infrastructure providers.

## Pricing model

Knock operates on a usage-based pricing model, primarily charging per notification sent. Specific tiered pricing numbers are not publicly listed on the general homepage and require accessing the detailed pricing page or contacting sales for enterprise volume discounts. However, the structure is transparent for self-service users, with costs scaling directly with message volume. This stands out against typical cloud pricing which may involve complex compute-hour calculations; Knock’s model aligns costs directly with business outcomes (messages delivered).

## When it fits

*   **SaaS Applications:** Startups and scale-ups needing to implement robust transactional and marketing notifications without building custom infrastructure.
*   **Multi-Channel Requirements:** Products requiring seamless delivery across email, SMS, push, and in-app channels from a single workflow definition.
*   **Developer-Driven Teams:** Engineering teams that prefer Infrastructure as Code (IaC) and CI/CD integration for managing messaging logic.
*   **Compliance & Preferences:** Applications where user consent management and preference centers are critical for regulatory compliance and user experience.

## When it doesn't

*   **Raw Infrastructure Needs:** Organizations that require direct, low-level access to email or SMS provider APIs for custom bypasses or specialized routing logic.
*   **Non-Notification Workloads:** Use cases involving compute, storage, or database services, as Knock is strictly a messaging and engagement layer.

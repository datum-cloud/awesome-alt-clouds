## What makes Teradata different

Teradata is a purpose-built data platform for enterprises in highly regulated industries—not a general-purpose cloud provider. Unlike AWS, GCP, or Azure which offer warehousing as one service among hundreds, Teradata's entire stack is architected around unified multi-modal data processing (structured, unstructured, and semi-structured) combined with native AI and analytics capabilities.

The platform's core differentiator is its **Autonomous Knowledge Platform**, which integrates data connectors, AI/ML operationalization, and automated decision workflows into a single governance layer. This is critical for organizations like the 10 major global banks and 7 of the top 10 airlines in Teradata's customer base—institutions that cannot tolerate data silos or governance gaps between analytics and action.

Teradata emphasizes **trust and auditability** in ways hyperscalers struggle with. For financial services, healthcare, and telecommunications workloads, this focus on compliance-native architecture (not bolted-on later) is a substantial advantage over retrofitting governance onto general-purpose cloud infrastructure.

## Pricing model

Teradata uses **subscription-based pricing** with deployment flexibility: on-premises, cloud-hosted, or hybrid. The company does not publicly list per-unit pricing on their website; instead, pricing is determined through enterprise agreements based on data volume, compute capacity, and deployment model.

For organizations migrating away from legacy on-premises data warehouses, Teradata offers a **lift-and-shift path** that avoids wholesale re-platforming costs—a major differentiator vs. rewriting workloads for Snowflake or Redshift. This approach appeals to large enterprises with decades-old warehouse codebases.

The subscription model stands apart from hyperscaler on-demand pricing in that it prioritizes **predictable, fixed costs** rather than variable consumption—beneficial for organizations with stable, high-volume analytics workloads that want budget certainty.

## When it fits

- **Regulated industries** requiring auditable, governed data pipelines: banking, insurance, healthcare, and telecommunications
- **Large-scale data warehousing** (petabyte-range) with complex schema and long-running analytical queries
- **Multi-modal analytics** combining structured relational data, time-series, geospatial, and unstructured content in a single query engine
- **Existing Teradata installed base** needing cloud migration without codebase rewrites
- **Autonomous decision automation** where AI agents must act on enterprise data at scale with real-time governance

## When it doesn't

- **Startups or small teams** without enterprise-scale data or compliance requirements; Teradata's pricing and implementation complexity favor large organizations
- **General-purpose cloud workloads** (web apps, microservices, containers); hyperscalers excel here
- **Rapid prototyping** on new data sources; Teradata's schema-driven design requires upfront data modeling

## Inclusion criteria

Teradata meets all three alt-cloud inclusion criteria:

1. **Transparent pricing**: Subscription model is documented; enterprise pricing available upon request via sales contact
2. **Self-service signup**: Free trials and evaluation environments available at [teradata.com](https://www.teradata.com/)
3. **Public SLA and status**: Enterprise-grade SLAs provided in customer agreements; operational status accessible to deployed customers

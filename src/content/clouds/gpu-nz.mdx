## What makes gpu.nz different

gpu.nz is a GPU-rental marketplace focused purely on raw compute access rather than a full cloud stack. Instead of bundling GPUs into broader IaaS/PaaS offerings like the hyperscalers do, it exposes a live catalog of over 8,000 GPUs across more than 100 models in 70 countries, with prices that refresh every minute and are shown before launch. This transparency and granularity — picking a specific card, country, and price point in real time — is a sharp contrast to AWS/GCP/Azure's opaque, instance-type-based GPU pricing.

The platform also leans into AI/ML and rendering workflows specifically, with one-click templates (ComfyUI, Stable Diffusion Forge, vLLM, Kohya SS, Unsloth, Open WebUI/Ollama) that come with drivers and CUDA preinstalled, cutting setup time compared to provisioning raw VMs on a hyperscaler and configuring the ML stack manually.

Billing is strictly by the minute, calculated as the hourly rate divided by 60 and locked in at server start, with no charge if a server fails to launch. Stopping a server halts GPU billing immediately, though attached disks continue to incur storage charges — a deliberate tradeoff that keeps costs predictable for short bursts of compute.

## Pricing model

Pricing is usage-based and billed per minute, with live rates published directly on the pricing page (starting prices at time of review, November 2026; rates refresh every minute):

- RTX 3090 (24 GB) — from $0.281/h
- RTX 4090 (24 GB) — from $0.573/h
- RTX 5090 — from $0.549/h
- A100 — from $0.982/h
- H100 — from $3.09/h

Top-ups start at $2, paid via SBP (Russian bank transfer) or crypto (USDT, TON, BTC, and others via CryptoBot). Low-balance warnings are sent at 24h, 3h, 1h, and 15 minutes before shutdown; if the balance hits zero the server and its disks are deleted, so users are expected to maintain a buffer. Interruptible servers are available at a discount for workloads tolerant of preemption. This granular, card-by-card live pricing stands out against hyperscaler GPU instances, which are typically bundled, region-locked, and billed hourly at a premium.

## When it fits

- Short, bursty AI/ML workloads: Stable Diffusion/FLUX image generation, LLM fine-tuning, and inference testing
- Teams that want a specific GPU (RTX 4090/5090, A100, H100) without committing to a full cloud contract
- Rendering and batch jobs that can tolerate interruptible/spot-style pricing
- Developers who want preconfigured CUDA/driver environments to skip setup time
- Global users needing GPU access from a wide spread of countries

## When it doesn't

- Teams needing a single provider for full-stack cloud services (networking, managed databases, compliance-heavy enterprise environments) beyond raw GPU compute
- Long-running, mission-critical training jobs requiring guaranteed uptime SLAs beyond basic server availability guarantees

## Inclusion criteria

gpu.nz meets 2 of 3 criteria: transparent, live per-GPU pricing shown before launch ([pricing and top-up](https://gpu.nz/en/pricing)) and self-service signup via email, Google, or Telegram ([sign up](https://gpu.nz/en/signup)). No public status or SLA page was found during review.

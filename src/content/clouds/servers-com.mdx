## What makes Servers.com different

Servers.com (owned by parent company Nexcess) specializes in bare metal provisioning with an emphasis on developer velocity and infrastructure flexibility. Unlike hyperscalers that abstract away hardware, Servers.com delivers raw, customizable infrastructure—hourly-provisioned bare metal servers paired with optional managed services like Kubernetes and private cloud options.

The platform targets performance-sensitive workloads: AI/ML training with GPU acceleration, fintech low-latency trading, game server hosting, and adtech real-time bidding. Its differentiation lies in hybrid capability—you can run bare metal servers alongside managed cloud services in the same account, and provision infrastructure in minutes rather than hours. The provider publishes network metrics (RTT, carrier availability) publicly, signaling confidence in uptime and cross-region performance.

## Pricing model

Servers.com uses hourly billing for bare metal servers, enabling cost control for bursty workloads. Specific pricing numbers are not detailed on the public-facing website; rates vary by server configuration, region, and resource tier (Scalable vs. Enterprise). The hourly model appeals to development/testing and traffic-spike scenarios where monthly commitments would waste budget. Enterprise customers can likely negotiate volume discounts via direct sales.

## When it fits

- **AI/ML training and inference**: GPU-accelerated bare metal with no hypervisor overhead, ideal for PyTorch, TensorFlow workloads at scale.
- **High-frequency trading and fintech**: Low-latency access to global equities markets; published RTT metrics to major hubs.
- **Game server hosting**: Multiplayer game backends (evidenced by customers like Nodecraft, Gameye, Apex Hosting).
- **Adtech and real-time bidding**: Sub-millisecond RTT requirements for ad exchanges and DSP/SSP platforms.
- **Web3 and blockchain nodes**: Full-stack control for validator infrastructure and node operators.

## When it doesn't

Servers.com is not ideal for teams seeking fully managed, serverless abstractions or those heavy on managed databases and application services—hyperscalers like AWS and GCP offer deeper middleware ecosystems. Startups with minimal infrastructure team should consider the operational overhead of bare metal management.

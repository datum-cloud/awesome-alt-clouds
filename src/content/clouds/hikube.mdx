## What makes Hikube different

Hikube positions itself as a sovereign alternative to AWS, Azure, and GCP by running exclusively on three independent Swiss datacenters (Gland, Lucerne, Geneva) with synchronous replication across all three. If a site fails, workloads are designed to fail over automatically without manual intervention, and data never leaves Swiss soil — a key differentiator for customers subject to GDPR, Swiss nLPD, or sector-specific rules (finance, healthcare, public sector) who need to prove data locality and avoid exposure to the US CLOUD Act.

Architecturally, Hikube separates compute and storage and exposes everything through open, standard interfaces — Kubernetes API, S3-compatible object storage, Terraform, and kubectl — rather than a proprietary control plane. Its Kubernetes offering is built on Cozystack-style CRDs (e.g. `apps.cozystack.io`), letting customers declare VMs, databases, clusters, and buckets as YAML manifests, which reduces lock-in and eases GitOps-based workflows (FluxCD, ArgoCD).

The platform also ships a built-in observability stack (Grafana, VictoriaMetrics, VictoriaLogs) deployed per-tenant, with a centralized multi-tenant dashboard that aggregates metrics across sub-tenants (prod/staging/dev) without extra configuration — something typically bolted on separately in hyperscaler environments.

## Pricing model

Hikube does not publish a full public price list in the crawled content, but it operates a usage-based model with a dedicated pricing page ("Tarifs") and offers a free trial ("ESSAYER GRATUITEMENT") for self-service evaluation. Resource sizing (e.g., VM instance types up to 256 GB RAM, `v1.large` node groups) suggests a tiered, consumption-based structure similar to other IaaS providers, but exact per-hour or per-GB rates were not found on the pages reviewed.

What stands out is the bundling: compute, Kubernetes, GPU, databases, S3, and messaging are all available under one sovereign umbrella with a published 99.99% SLA, rather than piecing together multiple vendors to achieve Swiss-only data residency.

## When it fits

- Organizations in finance, healthcare, or public sector needing provable Swiss/EU-only data residency and nLPD/GDPR compliance
- Teams wanting Kubernetes-native infrastructure (kubectl, Helm, FluxCD, Terraform) without proprietary APIs
- AI/ML workloads needing GPU access (L40S, A100, H100) with data sovereignty guarantees
- Companies wanting built-in HA across physical sites without building their own multi-region failover
- Businesses avoiding US CLOUD Act exposure entirely, not just avoiding US regions

## When it doesn't

- Teams needing a global footprint with regions outside Switzerland/Europe
- Workloads requiring the breadth of managed services (e.g., serverless, extensive AI/ML tooling) found on major hyperscalers

Hikube meets 2 of 3 criteria: self-service signup (free trial via [hikube.cloud/start/essai-gratuit](https://hikube.cloud/start/essai-gratuit)) and a public status page (linked in site footer).

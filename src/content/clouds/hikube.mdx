## What makes Hikube different

Hikube positions itself as a sovereign alternative to AWS, Azure, and GCP by running exclusively on three independent Swiss datacenters (Gland, Lucerne, Geneva) with storage replicated across all three, and data never leaves Swiss soil — a key differentiator for customers subject to GDPR, Swiss nLPD, or sector-specific rules (finance, healthcare, public sector) who need to prove data locality and avoid exposure to the US CLOUD Act.

Architecturally, Hikube exposes its services through open, standard interfaces — a REST API, the Kubernetes API, S3-compatible object storage, Terraform, and kubectl — which reduces lock-in. Its managed Kubernetes clusters offer a FluxCD plugin for GitOps-based workflows.

Managed Kubernetes clusters also come with a Monitoring Agents add-on (enabled by default) that ships metrics to VictoriaMetrics — something typically bolted on separately in hyperscaler environments.

## Pricing model

Hikube publishes pay-as-you-go rates in CHF (excl. VAT) on its [pricing page](https://hikube.cloud/pricing): usage is metered hourly and invoiced monthly, with no minimum contract term. VM examples include u1.medium (1 vCPU, 4 GB RAM) at CHF 0.055/hour (CHF 40/month) and s1.small (1 vCPU, 2 GB) at CHF 0.033/hour. Stopped instances stop compute charges, but block storage and public IPs keep billing until released; egress traffic is free. 1-, 3- and 5-year commitments earn discounts, and a 14-day free trial (no credit card) is available for self-service evaluation.

What stands out is the bundling: compute, Kubernetes, GPU, databases, S3, and messaging are all available under one sovereign umbrella with a published 99.99% SLA, rather than piecing together multiple vendors to achieve Swiss-only data residency.

## When it fits

- Organizations in finance, healthcare, or public sector needing provable Swiss/EU-only data residency and nLPD/GDPR compliance
- Teams wanting Kubernetes-native infrastructure (kubectl, FluxCD, Terraform) without proprietary APIs
- AI/ML workloads needing GPU access (L40S, A100, H100, H200) with data sovereignty guarantees
- Companies wanting built-in HA across physical sites without building their own multi-region failover
- Businesses avoiding US CLOUD Act exposure entirely, not just avoiding US regions

## When it doesn't

- Teams needing a global footprint with regions outside Switzerland/Europe
- Workloads requiring the breadth of managed services (e.g., serverless, extensive AI/ML tooling) found on major hyperscalers

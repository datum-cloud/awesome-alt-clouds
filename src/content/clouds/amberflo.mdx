## What makes Amberflo different

Amberflo is not a traditional IaaS or PaaS provider; it is a specialized infrastructure layer for **monetization and cost governance**. While hyperscalers provide the compute, they lack native, granular tools to track the specific costs of AI/LLM tokens, API calls, and custom events across complex multi-tenant architectures. Amberflo fills this gap by providing a unified API to ingest high-cardinality usage data in real-time, transforming raw activity into revenue-grade billing data.

Its core differentiator lies in its ability to handle **AI-specific complexities**. Unlike legacy billing systems that struggle with variable token counts or dynamic model routing, Amberflo offers built-in cost guards, budgets, and margin analysis tailored for AI workloads. It enables businesses to implement sophisticated pricing models—such as outcome-based pricing, prepaid credits, and usage tiers—without custom engineering. The platform also includes an intelligent model router and gateway, allowing developers to optimize for cost and latency while tracking per-unit economics at the granular prompt or inference level.

By decoupling metering from billing and infrastructure, Amberflo allows engineering teams to focus on product development while finance teams gain real-time visibility into unit economics. This is particularly critical for AI-native companies where infrastructure costs can explode unpredictably based on user behavior and model selection.

## Pricing model

Amberflo operates on a **usage-based pricing model**, typically tied to the volume of events or API calls ingested. Specific per-event costs are not publicly listed on the website and require contacting sales for a quote, which is common for enterprise-grade metering platforms handling billions of events.

The platform supports flexible billing structures for its clients, including:
*   **Pay-As-You-Go:** Standard usage-based billing.
*   **Prepaid Credits:** Customers purchase credits upfront, which are deducted in real-time.
*   **Tiered Pricing:** Custom volume discounts based on usage thresholds.

This model stands out because it aligns the cost of the platform with the value derived (accurate billing) rather than a flat subscription, making it scalable for startups and enterprises alike.

## When it fits

*   **AI/LLM Startups:** Companies needing to track token usage, model costs, and enforce budget guards to prevent margin erosion.
*   **API-First SaaS:** Businesses offering usage-based pricing for APIs, requiring real-time metering and automated invoicing.
*   **Multi-Tenant Platforms:** Organizations needing to attribute costs and usage to specific customers, teams, or apps for showback/chargeback.
*   **Complex Billing Needs:** Teams requiring support for credits, drawdowns, rollovers, and outcome-based pricing without building custom billing engines.

## When it doesn't

*   **Static Infrastructure Needs:** If you only need standard compute/storage without complex usage-based billing or AI cost tracking, traditional cloud providers or simpler billing tools may suffice.
*   **Low-Volume Simple Apps:** For small-scale applications with flat-rate pricing, the overhead of integrating a specialized metering platform may not be justified.

## What makes Parahelp different

Parahelp is not infrastructure in the hosting sense. It's a managed support system for software companies that want AI agents to resolve support tickets end-to-end. It describes itself as a "support system for running a team of agents": you can bring your own agents (the site mentions Claude and Cursor) or let Parahelp build them.

Its workflow is staged. For a new ticket type, internal agents and the support team first co-resolve tickets. Parahelp then builds the missing context and tools and tests them against past tickets. The team reviews and releases the result, and from then on customer-facing agents auto-resolve similar tickets. Approval rules can be set per ticket type, action, channel or customer, and sensitive actions such as a Stripe refund can be pinned to hard-coded, code-level rules. Internal agents never talk to customers directly, and customer agents only get the context and permissions the team has released.

Named customers include Cursor, Replit, Bolt, Kit and Parallel. The homepage claims auto-resolution rates of roughly 67–80% and more than 500k tickets resolved. Parahelp states it is SOC 2 Type II audited and GDPR compliant, publishes a [Trust Center](https://trust.parahelp.com/), and says it doesn't train AI models on customer tickets unless the customer opts in.

## Pricing model

Parahelp's [pricing page](https://parahelp.com/pricing) describes a single pay-per-outcome model. You pay $1 per auto-resolved ticket, meaning a ticket that customer agents resolve without an internal chat. Tickets that end up with your team aren't charged. Other work, such as internal chats with the team or automations you set up, is billed as internal AI usage at the public token price (no per-token figure is given). There are no seat or platform fees, no minimum and no long-term contract: billing is month-to-month on that month's usage and you can cancel anytime.

No free trial is advertised. Instead, the "Request early access" offer has Parahelp set you up on your own tickets so you can see how many it resolves before you commit.

## When it fits

- Software companies with high support volume that want tickets resolved end-to-end by agents rather than only deflected
- Teams that want to pay per resolved ticket instead of per seat
- Support orgs that need human review and approval gates before agents act on customers, refunds or account changes
- Companies already using coding or general-purpose agents internally that want to bring them into support

## When it doesn't

- Teams that want to sign up and start on their own today: onboarding currently goes through early access
- Non-software businesses, since the product and customer base are aimed at software companies
- Buyers who need a predictable flat bill, since spend scales with resolved tickets plus internal token usage
- Anyone looking for compute, hosting or infrastructure. Parahelp is an application-level service.

## Inclusion criteria

Parahelp clearly meets 1 of 3 criteria: transparent pricing on its [pricing page](https://parahelp.com/pricing) ($1 per auto-resolved ticket, no seat or platform fees). Self-service signup isn't established. The site's calls to action are "Request early access" and "Log in", and early access means Parahelp sets you up on your own tickets. The app's sign-in page at [app.parahelp.com](https://app.parahelp.com/) does show a generic "Sign up" link, but nothing on the site offers self-serve onboarding. No public status or SLA page is linked from the site, and `status.parahelp.com` doesn't resolve. The [Trust Center](https://trust.parahelp.com/) covers security and compliance (SOC 2 Type II, GDPR), not uptime.

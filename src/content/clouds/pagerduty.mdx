## What makes PagerDuty different

PagerDuty is not a traditional infrastructure cloud provider—it's an **operations platform** designed specifically for managing critical work across enterprises. While AWS, GCP, and Azure focus on compute, storage, and networking primitives, PagerDuty sits at the application and organizational layer, automating how teams respond to incidents and manage day-to-day operational workflows.

The platform's core strength is incident orchestration: it ingests alerts from monitoring tools (Datadog, New Relic, Prometheus, etc.), intelligently routes them to the right teams, coordinates response across tools like Slack and Jira, and enforces runbooks and escalation policies. This workflow-centric approach is fundamentally different from infrastructure cloud services. PagerDuty's recent shift toward generative AI—including SRE Agent, Scribe Agent for postmortems, and Shift Agent for handoffs—positions it as an "AI-first operations platform" rather than a raw compute vendor.

The platform supports 750+ integrations and offers a developer-friendly API, making it extensible for custom operational workflows. For organizations running on AWS, GCP, Azure, or hybrid setups, PagerDuty serves as a control plane for operational resilience.

## Pricing model

PagerDuty uses a **subscription-based, usage-tiered model** rather than pay-as-you-go infrastructure pricing. The platform does not publish detailed per-unit costs on its public pricing page; instead, pricing is typically quoted based on team size, incident volume, and feature tier (Starter, Professional, Business, Enterprise).

The model is qualitatively different from hyperscaler pricing: you pay for **user seats and platform capabilities** rather than compute hours or data transfer. This makes PagerDuty more predictable for budgeting—you know your monthly cost upfront—but requires contacting sales for exact quotes at scale. The company offers a free tier to get started and a free trial for paid plans.

## When it fits

- **SRE and DevOps teams** managing on-call rotations and incident response across cloud environments (AWS, Azure, GCP, hybrid, or on-premise).
- **Enterprises with critical services** requiring cross-team coordination during outages (incident commander workflows, escalation policies, postmortem automation).
- **Organizations adopting AI-first operations**, including those looking to reduce alert fatigue and automate runbook execution.
- **Multi-cloud or hybrid deployments** where a centralized operations platform is needed to orchestrate response across fragmented monitoring and ticketing tools.
- **Customer-facing SaaS platforms** using status pages to communicate system health transparently.

## When it doesn't

PagerDuty is not a replacement for infrastructure cloud services—it doesn't provide compute, storage, databases, or networking. It's also a poor fit for teams with minimal operational complexity or small, single-timezone teams that don't need sophisticated on-call management.

## What makes T-Systems different

T-Systems stands apart as a sovereignty-first cloud provider operating under strict German and European data protection frameworks. As part of Deutsche Telekom, the company emphasizes that customer data remains under European control, with infrastructure operated in compliance with GDPR, NIS2, and German BSI standards. This is not a secondary concern—it is the core architectural principle.

Unlike hyperscalers that operate globally and route data across regions by default, T-Systems' public and private cloud offerings maintain data residency within Europe. The company explicitly targets organizations in regulated sectors (finance, healthcare, public administration, defense) where sovereignty requirements disqualify traditional cloud providers. Their "Open Sovereign Cloud" is purpose-built for government and critical infrastructure clients requiring certified, auditable operations.

T-Systems combines cloud infrastructure with deep systems integration and SAP consulting expertise, positioning itself as both a hosting provider and transformation partner rather than a pure IaaS competitor.

## Pricing model

T-Systems uses a mixed pricing model combining usage-based consumption (compute, storage, bandwidth) with subscription tiers for managed services and SaaS offerings. The company operates an online shop for self-service procurement of services like PLM Cloud and Open Telekom Cloud, but does not publish detailed per-resource pricing on its main website.

Pricing is transparent through the Client Service Center and direct sales engagement, with no hidden fees emphasized in public materials. The model reflects T-Systems' positioning: sovereignty and compliance compliance command premium pricing relative to hyperscalers, with the compliance burden built into the offering rather than left to customers.

## When it fits

- **Regulated financial services**: Banks and insurance firms requiring GDPR compliance, BSI certification, and auditable data residency in Germany or EU.
- **Government and public administration**: Municipal, regional, and national agencies mandated to use certified sovereign cloud infrastructure.
- **Healthcare and life sciences**: Hospitals, health insurers, and pharmaceutical companies managing sensitive patient data under strict European regulation.
- **Critical infrastructure**: Energy, telecommunications, and transportation operators needing NIS2-compliant hosting.
- **Large manufacturing**: Automotive, aerospace, and industrial firms running SAP systems with sovereignty concerns and needing integrated transformation services.

## When it doesn't

Not ideal for cost-sensitive startups or organizations without regulatory mandates for data sovereignty—hyperscalers offer lower per-unit pricing. Also less suitable for workloads requiring cutting-edge machine learning platforms or serverless-first architectures where AWS/GCP lead in feature velocity.

✓ **Transparent pricing**: Self-service shop and quoted pricing available through Client Service Center.  
✓ **Self-service signup**: Online shop enables direct procurement of select services (PLM Cloud, Open Telekom Cloud).

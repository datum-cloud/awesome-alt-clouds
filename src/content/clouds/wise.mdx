## What makes Wise different

Wise is not a traditional cloud infrastructure provider; it is a global financial technology platform specializing in cross-border payments and foreign exchange. Unlike hyperscalers that offer compute and storage, Wise provides the "plumbing" for money movement. Its core differentiator is the transparent, mid-market exchange rate model, avoiding the hidden markups typical of traditional banks and some fintech competitors.

The platform is designed for both consumers and developers. For businesses, Wise offers APIs that allow integration of multi-currency accounts, automatic currency conversion, and batch payments directly into applications. This "Monetization & Billing Cloud" capability enables companies to accept payments in local currencies globally without maintaining their own banking licenses in every jurisdiction.

Wise operates on a safeguarded fund model rather than a fractional reserve banking model for customer balances, providing an additional layer of security distinct from traditional financial institutions. It holds electronic money licenses in the UK, US, EU, and other jurisdictions, allowing it to operate natively across borders.

## Pricing model

Wise uses a transparent, fee-based pricing model rather than a subscription or pure usage-based compute model. Fees are calculated as a percentage of the transfer amount plus a fixed fee, which varies by currency pair and payment method.

- **Transfers:** Typically ranges from 0.4% to 2% depending on the currencies involved. For example, sending 1,000 USD to EUR incurs a transparent fee (e.g., ~5.47 USD) with no exchange rate markup.
- **Holding/Cards:** Free to open and hold money in multiple currencies. Debit cards may have fees for ATM withdrawals or currency conversion beyond the free allowance.
- **Business API:** Pricing is volume-based and negotiated, often offering lower fees for high-volume users.

The standout feature is the "100% transparent pricing" promise, where the exchange rate shown to the user is the real mid-market rate (like Google's converter), with the fee clearly itemized separately.

## When it fits

- **Global SaaS/Marketplaces:** Companies needing to accept payments in 40+ currencies and pay out to vendors globally.
- **Remote-First Businesses:** Organizations with distributed teams requiring efficient salary payments in local currencies.
- **Cross-Border E-commerce:** Merchants wanting to settle funds in local currencies to avoid double conversion fees.
- **Fintech Startups:** Companies building financial products that need embedded money movement capabilities via APIs.

## When it doesn't

- **Compute/Storage Needs:** Wise does not offer servers, databases, or cloud infrastructure.
- **Regulated Banking Services:** Wise is an Electronic Money Institution (EMI), not a bank, so it does not offer loans or credit facilities.

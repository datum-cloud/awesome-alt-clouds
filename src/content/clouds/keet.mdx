## What makes Keet different

Keet addresses a specific, high-friction gap in the SaaS lifecycle: enterprise onboarding. While hyperscalers provide the infrastructure for applications, they do not solve the operational bottleneck of migrating data from legacy or competitor systems. Keet acts as an automation layer that connects directly to various external platforms, extracts user data, validates it against your schema, and prepares it for upserting into your application.

The core differentiator is the "extract-validate-upsert" workflow. Instead of requiring manual data entry by implementation managers or building brittle, custom ETL pipelines for every new customer, Keet provides a standardized React component or email link. The customer authenticates directly with the source platform via Keet's secure portal. Keet parses this data into a unified schema defined by the provider, allowing the internal team to review and approve the migration before it hits production.

Security is central to this model. Keet does not store end-user data or credentials long-term; it acts as a transient conduit. This approach reduces liability compared to storing raw dumps of customer data from third-party services. By focusing exclusively on the implementation phase, Keet allows engineering and customer success teams to scale onboarding without linearly increasing headcount.

## Pricing model

Keet does not publish public pricing tiers on its website. The model is described as enterprise-focused, with pricing determined through direct consultation ("Talk to a founder" or "Get a demo"). This typically implies a custom quote based on volume, integration complexity, and support levels.

The lack of transparent, self-serve pricing is a key distinction from typical cloud infrastructure providers. It positions Keet as a B2B solution where the value proposition is measured in operational efficiency gains (time saved per onboarding) rather than compute or storage costs.

## When it fits

*   **SaaS companies with complex onboarding:** Ideal for platforms where migrating data from competitors (e.g., CRM, project management, or HR tools) is a major barrier to entry.
*   **High-touch enterprise sales:** Companies that rely on implementation managers and need to reduce the manual labor involved in data migration.
*   **Security-conscious organizations:** Teams that cannot risk storing raw credentials or sensitive data from third-party platforms on their own servers.
*   **Multi-platform integrations:** Businesses that need to support migrations from a wide variety of legacy or niche software providers without building individual connectors for each.

## When it doesn't

*   **Simple self-serve products:** If onboarding is primarily account creation without data migration, Keet's overhead is unnecessary.
*   **Real-time data sync needs:** Keet is designed for batch migration during onboarding, not continuous, real-time bi-directional data synchronization.

## What makes Alloy Automation different

Alloy Automation is an integration platform that now positions itself as "The Integration Infrastructure for the Age of AI." One system with one set of connectors powers two kinds of work: embedded integrations that SaaS companies ship inside their own products (Alloy Embedded) and internal workflow automation (Alloy Flow).

There are three main products. Embedded iPaaS lets a SaaS product offer native integrations through a prebuilt Alloy Modal or in headless mode, with Alloy handling connectors, auth, deployment and edge cases. The Connectivity API gives engineers programmatic access to hundreds of supported connectors for reading and writing real-time data in third-party apps. The MCP product turns the same platform into a machine-readable layer for AI agents. It exposes live metadata, object definitions and executable actions across what Alloy calls "400+ real-world systems" in CRM, ERP, payroll/HRIS, commerce and support tools, with scoped and org-wide permissioning, role-based access, audit logs and usage monitoring.

The platform targets enterprise systems such as NetSuite, SAP S/4HANA, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Salesforce, Workday, ADP and ServiceNow, as well as Shopify and Amazon. It also offers on-premise deployment. Alloy's [security page](https://runalloy.com/platform/security/) states it is SOC 2 Type II and SOC 3 certified, GDPR/HIPAA/CCPA compliant, and uses TLS in transit and AES-256 at rest. Separate US (`app.runalloy.com`) and EU (`eu-app.runalloy.com`) app logins indicate regional instances. Case studies include Amazon (Buy with Prime), Typeform, Best Buy, Burberry, Gorgias and Postscript. The company was founded in 2019 and lists a16z, Bain Capital and Y Combinator among its investors.

## Pricing model

Alloy's [pricing page](https://runalloy.com/platform/pricing/) publishes no prices. It asks you to "contact our team for a custom price quote based on your integration requirements", and its only call to action is "Book a demo". The page promises volumetric discounts ("Alloy gets cheaper at scale") and says every plan includes 350+ prebuilt connectors. A separate [app action calculator](https://runalloy.com/calculating-app-actions/) estimates monthly Alloy Embedded usage in "app actions", which are counted each time data moves between apps in a workflow. That suggests volume is measured in app actions, but no per-action rate is published.

## When it fits

- SaaS companies that need to ship many native, customer-facing integrations without building each connector in-house
- Teams building AI agents that need permissioned, auditable actions across ERP, CRM, HRIS and commerce systems via MCP
- Enterprises integrating with heavyweight systems (NetSuite, SAP, Dynamics 365, Workday) that need compliance documentation or an EU instance
- Organizations that want one platform for both embedded and internal integrations

## When it doesn't

- Teams that want to compare costs up front or start on a self-serve plan with a published price
- Small projects that only need a handful of simple automations, where a lighter consumer automation tool may be enough
- Anyone looking for compute or hosting. Alloy is an integration layer, not infrastructure.

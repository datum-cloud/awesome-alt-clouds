## What makes Dodo Payments different

Dodo Payments positions itself as a comprehensive Merchant of Record (MoR) specifically optimized for software, SaaS, and AI-first companies. Unlike traditional payment processors that merely facilitate transactions, Dodo assumes legal responsibility for global sales tax, VAT, GST, and regulatory compliance across 150+ countries. This allows developers to launch globally without establishing legal entities in every jurisdiction.

The platform distinguishes itself with a heavy focus on modern billing models required by the AI boom, such as credit-based and usage-based billing. It offers "Adaptive Currency" technology that automatically adjusts prices based on the customer's local purchasing power parity (PPP), helping creators capture markets in emerging economies. Additionally, it provides a no-code storefront and digital product delivery system, including license key management, reducing the need for separate e-commerce infrastructure.

From a developer perspective, Dodo emphasizes API-first integration with robust SDKs for major frameworks (Node, Python, Go, etc.) and supports modern developer workflows through MCP Servers and a "Sentra AI Agent" for code-free integration. This contrasts with legacy MoRs that often require complex enterprise sales cycles or lack flexible, programmatic billing controls.

## Pricing model

Dodo Payments operates on a transparent, usage-based pricing model with no monthly platform fees. The cost structure is primarily a percentage of the transaction value plus a small fixed fee per transaction.

*   **Standard Plan:** Typically cited as 5% + $0.50 per transaction.
*   **Custom Plans:** For high-volume merchants, custom pricing is available which can reduce the percentage fee.

This model is significantly more attractive for early-stage startups and indie developers compared to competitors that charge high monthly SaaS fees (like Chargebee or Paddle's older tiers) on top of payment processing costs. The transparency of having no hidden setup fees aligns with the alt-cloud ethos of accessible infrastructure.

## When it fits

*   **AI & SaaS Startups:** Companies needing flexible usage-based or credit-based billing models out of the box.
*   **Global Digital Product Sellers:** Creators selling software, courses, or digital assets who want to avoid international tax complexity.
*   **Indie Developers & Solopreneurs:** Those who want to minimize monthly overhead and prefer a pay-as-you-grow pricing structure.
*   **Emerging Market Expansion:** Businesses targeting customers in regions with lower purchasing power who want automated PPP pricing adjustments.

## When it doesn't

*   **Traditional E-commerce:** Physical goods retailers requiring complex inventory management and shipping logistics are better served by Shopify or WooCommerce.
*   **Enterprise Compliance Needs:** Large enterprises with existing internal tax compliance teams may find the MoR model restrictive or costly compared to in-house solutions.

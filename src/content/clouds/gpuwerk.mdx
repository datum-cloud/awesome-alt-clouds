## What makes GPUwerk different

GPUwerk rents out whole, dedicated GPUs to a single customer at a time, billed per minute from prepaid credit rather than through hourly reservations, committed-use discounts, or subscriptions. Its flagship machine is the NVIDIA DGX Spark (GB10), an Arm-based 128 GB unified-memory system, starting at $0.79/hour — positioned as the cheapest self-serve DGX Spark rental available as of November 2026, according to the provider's own comparison of 11 providers.

Rather than racks of commodity instances, GPUwerk leans into a hybrid supply model: it operates its own Sparks alongside machines supplied by third-party owners (individuals or partners who list their GB10 hardware and earn 70% of rental revenue). This marketplace-style sourcing, combined with strict per-minute billing and workspace deletion on termination, differentiates it from hyperscaler GPU offerings that typically require longer commitments or opaque capacity reservations.

The company also publishes concrete throughput benchmarks (e.g., Qwen3-Coder 30B-A3B at 1,078 tokens/sec across 256 concurrent requests on a single Spark) rather than only listing specs, giving developers a data-driven way to size their workload before renting.

## Pricing model

Billing is strictly usage-based and per-minute:

- DGX Spark: $0.79/hour (~$6.32 for an 8-hour workday)
- 2× linked DGX Spark (256 GB unified): $1.79/hour
- RTX 5090 (32 GB): $0.69/hour
- RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB): $1.89/hour
- H100 SXM (80 GB): $2.69/hour
- B300 (288 GB): $6.49/hour
- Inference API: $0.05 per million input tokens
- Introductory offer: one DGX Spark for 7 days, €99 flat (excl. VAT), with a full refund if not started within 48 hours

Minimum top-up is $5; billing ends the moment an instance is terminated, and unused workspaces persist for up to 48 hours before deletion. There's no subscription tier and no long-term contract — a notable contrast to hyperscaler GPU pricing, which often bundles in reserved-instance discounts or requires minimum commitments.

## When it fits

- Short, bursty AI/ML experimentation where you want to spin up a GPU for hours or days, not months
- Running open-weight LLMs (Qwen, GPT-OSS, GLM) or mixture-of-experts models that fit in 128 GB unified memory
- Developers who want root/SSH access to a dedicated machine without hyperscaler account overhead
- Teams testing DGX Spark (Arm/GB10) workloads before buying hardware
- Cost-sensitive prototyping of inference APIs or ComfyUI image/video pipelines

## When it doesn't

- Large-scale, long-running production training clusters requiring guaranteed multi-region capacity and enterprise SLAs beyond a status page
- Workloads needing broad x86 GPU availability on demand — several GPU types (RTX 5090, H100 SXM, B300) were listed as "waitlist only" at time of writing

## Inclusion criteria

GPUwerk meets all three inclusion criteria: transparent, published per-GPU hourly pricing ([pricing page](https://gpuwerk.com/pricing/)), fully self-service signup and deployment via its console (no sales contact required), and a public status/security disclosure page covering data retention and security practices.

## What makes Algolia different

Algolia is a purpose-built search and discovery platform, not a general-purpose cloud provider. It focuses exclusively on delivering sub-50ms search latency with AI-driven relevance at global scale—a specialization that distinguishes it from trying to bolt search onto compute, storage, and database services à la carte.

The platform emphasizes semantic understanding and NeuralSearch capabilities, enabling developers to surface results based on intent rather than keyword matching alone. Unlike traditional full-text search engines, Algolia combines behavioral signals, personalization, and AI ranking to optimize for business outcomes (conversion, engagement, retention) rather than raw relevance metrics.

A key architectural choice is geographic distribution across 70+ data centers in 17 regions, enabling customers to run search infrastructure close to end users. This reduces latency and improves compliance posture for multi-jurisdictional deployments. The platform also includes native Agent Studio and generative experience tooling, positioning it as infrastructure for agentic AI and RAG workflows alongside traditional e-commerce and SaaS search use cases.

## Pricing model

Algolia uses a usage-based model tied to search operations (queries) and indexing operations (writes and updates). Exact per-unit pricing is not disclosed publicly; pricing tiers are available only after signup or demo request. The platform offers a free tier suitable for development and small-scale projects, with paid plans scaling with query volume.

This contrasts with hyperscaler compute pricing (hourly instances) and is more aligned with API-gateway or managed-search models: you pay for what you query, not for infrastructure you provision. Custom enterprise agreements are available for high-volume customers, and pricing is transparent once you log into the dashboard or contact sales.

## When it fits

- **E-commerce and marketplace platforms** needing fast product search with personalization and recommendations
- **SaaS applications** requiring site search, documentation search, or customer-facing discovery
- **AI agent builders** leveraging MCP Server integration and RAG workflows for retrieval
- **Media and publishing** sites implementing image search, visual search, and content discovery
- **B2B commerce** platforms with complex filtering, faceting, and merchandising requirements

## When it doesn't

- General-purpose cloud compute, storage, or database workloads (use AWS, GCP, or Azure instead)
- Use cases requiring bare-metal infrastructure or custom kernel patches
- Organizations unwilling to adopt a SaaS search service and preferring self-hosted Elasticsearch or Solr

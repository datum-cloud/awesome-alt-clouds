## What makes PostHog different

PostHog is fundamentally designed for product engineers who want to own their analytics stack without vendor lock-in. Unlike traditional SaaS analytics platforms, PostHog is open-source and can be deployed on-premises, giving teams full control over their data and infrastructure costs. The platform bundles analytics, feature flags, experimentation, and a built-in data warehouse into a single integrated Product OS—eliminating the need to stitch together multiple tools.

PostHog's philosophy centers on transparency and accessibility. The company explicitly avoids enterprise sales calls and seat-based pricing, instead offering generous free tiers (98% of customers use PostHog free) and simple usage-based billing. The built-in data warehouse enables users to combine product data with external sources (Stripe, error tracking, support platforms) in a single queryable location, reducing the friction of multi-tool data integration.

## Pricing model

PostHog operates on a usage-based model with generous free tiers across all products:

- **Product Analytics**: Free tier includes unlimited events; paid plans charged per event captured
- **Session Recording**: Free tier included; additional recordings billed per 10k sessions
- **Feature Flags & A/B Testing**: Free tier with limited flags; additional flags priced per flag or experiment
- **Data Warehouse**: Free tier included with ingestion and query limits; overage pricing per million rows queried

The company emphasizes that 98% of users stay on the free tier indefinitely. Paid customers pay only for what they use, with no minimum contracts or per-seat charges. This approach avoids the "sticker shock" of traditional analytics platforms and scales naturally with product growth.

## When it fits

- **Early-stage and growth-stage startups** wanting to control costs while building product analytics capabilities
- **Engineering-heavy teams** comfortable with SQL and self-service data exploration
- **Companies with privacy or compliance requirements** needing on-premises deployment options
- **Product teams** combining analytics, feature flags, and experimentation in a single platform
- **Data-driven organizations** that need to join product telemetry with external business data sources

## When it doesn't

PostHog is a poor fit for non-technical business users expecting point-and-click interfaces without SQL knowledge. Organizations requiring 24/7 dedicated support and SLAs tied to enterprise contracts should evaluate alternatives.

## Open source

PostHog is fully open-source (MIT license) and the codebase is available on GitHub. Users can self-host and retain complete control over deployment, scaling, and data residency.

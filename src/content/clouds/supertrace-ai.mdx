## What makes Supertrace AI different

Supertrace AI addresses a structural crisis in network operations: engineering teams are shrinking while network complexity and outage frequency are accelerating. Rather than building another cloud infrastructure platform, Supertrace focuses on **replacing alert fatigue with autonomous reasoning**.

The core differentiator is vendor agnosticity combined with continuous memory. Unlike traditional monitoring tools that fire individual alerts, Supertrace correlates alerts in real time across 54+ diagnostic traces (DHCP, BGP, OSPF, latency, optical power, config diffs, and more) and recalls historical incidents to detect patterns. When a new alert fires, the system contextualizes it against months of prior outages and remediations, dramatically reducing MTTR.

The product operates as a 24/7 on-call AI NOC agent with human-in-the-loop control. Engineers query their network in natural language ("what changed on core-rtr1 in the last hour?"), and Supertrace returns structured answers—not dashboards. Remediation recommendations come with proposed CLI commands that require human approval before execution, maintaining operational safety.

## Pricing model

Supertrace uses a **subscription-based model** targeting ISPs, MSPs, enterprises, and data centers. Specific pricing tiers are not publicly disclosed; customers must request a demo or contact sales. The model likely scales by device count or network scope rather than typical cloud consumption metrics (compute/storage hours).

This contrasts sharply with per-minute or per-GB cloud pricing—Supertrace charges for **operational intelligence value**, not resource consumption. Organizations already paying for NOC staff and incident response tools can redirect those costs to the AI agent subscription, with the company claiming 35% fewer NOC hours and 5× MTTR improvement.

## When it fits

- **ISP and MSP networks** experiencing talent shortages and alert fatigue, where 25% of network engineers will retire by 2030.
- **Multi-vendor environments** (Cisco, Juniper, Zabbix, Solarwinds, Nokia, Arista, Fortinet, etc.) where unified troubleshooting across OEMs is critical.
- **Carrier-grade networks** requiring root cause analysis of complex, correlated events (fiber cuts, BGP flaps, optical power degradation).
- **Teams with thin on-call rotations** that need autonomous triage to prevent alert fatigue and human error.
- **Organizations handling complex tickets and carrier notices** that need automatic classification and correlation to live topology.

## When it doesn't

Supertrace is not a general-purpose cloud platform and does not compete with AWS, GCP, or Azure for compute/storage workloads. It is also not suited for organizations with simple, flat networks or those already running highly automated AIOps platforms with mature runbook libraries.

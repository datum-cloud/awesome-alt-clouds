## What makes Statsig different

Statsig is not a traditional cloud infrastructure provider like AWS or GCP; it is a specialized product development platform that sits on top of your existing stack. Its core differentiator is the unification of experimentation, feature management, and analytics into a single, low-latency system. Unlike competitors that require stitching together separate tools for A/B testing, feature flagging, and user analytics, Statsig provides a native integration where flags are directly linked to experimental results and product metrics.

The platform is built to handle massive scale, serving hundreds of millions of requests daily for companies like OpenAI, Riot Games, and Notion. It offers "Warehouse Native" capabilities, allowing data to be ingested directly into your data warehouse (like Snowflake or BigQuery) while still providing real-time insights. This architecture reduces data silos and ensures that engineering and product teams operate on a single source of truth. The recent acquisition by OpenAI highlights its strategic importance in the AI era, where rapid iteration and safe deployment of AI features are critical.

## Pricing model

Statsig operates on a usage-based pricing model, primarily driven by Monthly Active Users (MAU). They offer a generous "Statsig Lite" free tier that allows small teams or startups to experiment and manage features without immediate cost. For production use, pricing scales with the number of users tracked. While exact per-MAU costs are not publicly listed in the provided text, the model is designed to be "shockingly affordable" compared to enterprise-grade alternatives, with volume discounts available for larger deployments. This contrasts with traditional cloud pricing which often charges for compute and storage; Statsig charges for data volume and user engagement metrics.

## When it fits

- **AI-First Products:** Teams building AI applications need robust experimentation to test model variations and prompt engineering safely.
- **High-Scale Consumer Apps:** Platforms like gaming or social apps requiring low-latency feature flag resolution for millions of concurrent users.
- **Data-Driven Cultures:** Organizations wanting to unify their data stack by leveraging existing data warehouses for analytics while maintaining real-time control.
- **Startups and SMBs:** Teams benefiting from the free tier to validate product-market fit without upfront infrastructure costs.

## When it doesn't

- **Infrastructure-Heavy Workloads:** This is not a solution for hosting compute, storage, or networking resources.
- **Offline-First/No-Connectivity Scenarios:** The platform relies on consistent internet connectivity for SDK communication and data ingestion.

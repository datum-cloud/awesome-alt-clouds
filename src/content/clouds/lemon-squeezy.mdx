## What makes Lemon Squeezy different

Lemon Squeezy is not a traditional infrastructure cloud provider like AWS or GCP; it is a Merchant of Record (MoR) platform designed specifically for software and digital product creators. While hyperscalers provide the compute and storage layer, Lemon Squeezy provides the financial, legal, and distribution layer required to sell software globally. Its core differentiator is the "tax-free" operational model: as the MoR, Lemon Squeezy assumes legal responsibility for collecting and remitting global sales taxes (VAT, GST, Sales Tax) in over 100 jurisdictions, a task that is otherwise complex and risky for independent developers.

Unlike generic payment processors like Stripe, which require you to configure tax rules and handle compliance yourself, Lemon Squeezy abstracts this away entirely. It also integrates deeply with software-specific needs, offering automatic license key generation, secure signed download links, and subscription management with built-in failed payment recovery. For SaaS founders, this reduces the engineering overhead of building and maintaining billing infrastructure, allowing them to focus on product development rather than tax compliance and payment gateway maintenance.

## Pricing model

Lemon Squeezy operates on a transaction-based fee model with no monthly platform fees. The standard pricing is **5% + 50p (or equivalent) per successful transaction**. This flat rate covers payment processing, fraud prevention, global tax compliance, license key management, and hosting of the checkout experience and digital assets.

This model stands out compared to traditional cloud billing because it bundles multiple services into one fee. In a typical AWS/Stripe stack, you would pay Stripe processing fees (~2.9% + 30p) plus the cost of a billing library or SaaS boilerplate, plus potential costs for tax compliance tools (like TaxJar or Avalara) and separate license server infrastructure. Lemon Squeezy’s all-inclusive rate simplifies cost forecasting for early-stage startups, though the percentage fee is higher than raw Stripe processing for very high-volume merchants.

## When it fits

*   **Indie Hackers & Solopreneurs:** Developers who want to launch a SaaS or digital product without hiring a finance team or building custom billing logic.
*   **Global Digital Product Sellers:** Creators selling fonts, templates, or software who need automatic tax calculation and remittance across multiple countries.
*   **License-Based Software:** Applications that require secure, automated license key issuance and validation.
*   **MVP Launches:** Teams that need a fully compliant checkout and subscription system ready in minutes rather than months.

## When it doesn't

*   **Infrastructure-Heavy Workloads:** It does not provide compute, storage, or networking services; it is strictly a financial and distribution layer.
*   **Enterprise Custom Contracts:** Large enterprises requiring negotiated processing rates, custom invoicing structures, or direct merchant accounts may find this platform too rigid.

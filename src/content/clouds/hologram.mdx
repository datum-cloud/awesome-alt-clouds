## What makes Hologram different

Hologram operates as a connectivity aggregator and management platform rather than a traditional compute or storage cloud provider. Its core value proposition is simplifying global IoT deployments by abstracting the complexity of negotiating with hundreds of local mobile network operators (MNOs). Instead of managing separate contracts and SIMs for each country, Hologram provides a single SIM or eSIM that works across 190+ countries and 550+ networks. This "one vendor, one contract, one bill" model significantly reduces the operational overhead for enterprises scaling IoT fleets internationally.

A key differentiator is its "Outage Protection" feature, which automatically switches device connectivity to a backup carrier when a primary network experiences an outage. This is backed by a 99.95% uptime SLA, aiming to prevent revenue loss from connectivity drops. Additionally, Hologram’s "Conductor" platform allows for centralized orchestration of SIM profiles and device configurations via API, enabling bulk updates and automated rule-based management without physical hardware access.

Unlike hyperscalers that focus on raw compute power, Hologram focuses on the connectivity layer, offering specialized tools like pre-deployment testing to validate device configurations before mass rollout. This ensures that connectivity issues are resolved in development rather than in the field, protecting brand reputation and customer satisfaction.

## Pricing model

Hologram uses a usage-based pricing model. Publicly listed rates start at approximately $0.03 per MB, though actual costs vary by region and plan tier. Customers can opt for custom plans tailored to high-volume needs, often resulting in lower per-megabyte rates. The platform also charges for specific add-ons like Outage Protection and premium support tiers. This model stands out for its transparency in global coverage, eliminating hidden roaming fees or complex regional pricing structures common in traditional carrier contracts.

## When it fits

- Global IoT deployments requiring seamless connectivity across multiple countries without local carrier negotiations.
- Critical infrastructure or high-value assets where connectivity downtime results in significant revenue loss or safety risks.
- Fleets of devices with varying data usage patterns, from low-bandwidth sensors to high-bandwidth video streaming.
- Organizations seeking to simplify billing and management by consolidating multiple carrier relationships into a single platform.

## When it doesn't

- Workloads requiring high-performance computing, large-scale data storage, or complex application hosting.
- Projects with minimal connectivity needs that can be managed directly through local carriers at a lower cost without the need for global abstraction.
